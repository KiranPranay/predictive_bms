1. How to use this guide

  • Build in the order of section 12, and follow the gates of the safe build sequence (section 14). No lithium cell is connected before Gate 3.
  • Holes are named by column and row, (c, r), counted from 0 at the top-left corner of the component side. Column 0 is on the left; on the underside drawings it is on the right, because the board is turned over.
  • Wire colours in the drawings mark nets, not the colour of wire to buy: red 3V3, orange 5 V, black GND, blue SDA, amber SCL, purple taps, green ADC inputs, teal NTC lines.
  • The board layouts are generated from one description of each board, which is checked automatically: every net is joined, and no two nets touch. The placement tables, wiring lists and continuity checks come from the same description.
  • Generic modules differ. Before soldering a module's header, compare its printed pin names with section 10.

2. The system at a glance

Two independent layers protect the pack. Layer A is a hardware BMS that always limits cell voltage and current and balances the cells. Layer B is the ESP32 supervisor: five times a second it measures every cell voltage, the pack current, four cell temperatures and the air temperature, runs a two-state thermal model of every cell with a Kalman filter, and opens separate charge and load relays, latched, before a fixed limit is reached.

3. Single-line diagram

  • The fuse sits at the pack's positive exit, then the shunt, then the branch wiring (section 11).
  • K_LOAD and K_CHARGE are normally-open contacts. Pull-ups hold both open whenever the ESP32 is reset, crashed or unpowered.
  • The LM2596 is fed after the fuse and before the shunt. Its own current therefore does not count as pack current, so the pack can read "at rest" (below 20 mA), which the self-discharge estimate needs.

8. Board A: cell-tap sensing

Four identical divider channels feed the ADS1115. Parts are placed by channel, left to right in the direction of the signal: tap in, 330 kΩ, the node, 10 kΩ and 56 kΩ, then the filter capacitor and the clamp. Two bare buses run down the board: GND at column 15 and 3V3 at column 17.

J1 pin 1 (B−) is deliberately not connected. Joining B− to this board's ground would join B− and P− through a thin wire and bypass the hardware BMS's switch.

12. Assembly, step by step

Board A

  1. Mark the top-left corner of the component side with a pen, so the hole coordinates stay the right way round.
  2. Fit and solder the low parts first: R1–R12, then C1–C4. Leave D1–D4 empty: the clamp part is chosen after the Gate 1 leakage test.
  3. Fit J1, the 1 × 10 female header J2 and the 4-pin header J3.
  4. Underside: lay the two long buses (GND column 15, 3V3 column 17) as bare wire, then every bare bridge of the wiring list, then the insulated links. Tick each line of the wiring list as you go.
  5. Clean the flux off with isopropyl alcohol: residue leaks current across the 330 kΩ dividers.
  6. Do the checks of section 13 before fitting the ADS1115.

Board B

  1. Fit R1–R8, C1–C5 and LED1 (flat side, the cathode, towards column 17).
  2. Fit the headers: two 1 × 15 female headers for the ESP32 (dry-fit the DevKit first: most have their rows 25.4 mm, 10 holes, apart), the 1 × 8 female header J8, J5, J6, J7, J14, the five 2-pin JST sockets and the screw terminal J4.
  3. Underside: the rails (3V3 along row 16 and column 21, GND along row 19 and column 25), then the bridges, then the insulated links.
  4. Clean the flux, then do the checks of section 13 before plugging in the ESP32 or the INA226.

Then, gate by gate

  1. Gate 1: plug in the ESP32, the ADS1115 and the INA226, join J3 to J7, flash the firmware, calibrate (section 14).
  2. Gate 2: set the LM2596 to 5.00 V with nothing connected and load-test it; then wire J4, the relay module (J5) and the buzzer (J6).
  3. Gates 3–8: the BMS harness, cell matching, welding and the first power-up, as the safe build sequence says.

13. Checks before power

17. Safety rules

  • No lithium cell is connected before Gate 3. The first live power-up and the first charge are never done alone.
  • Never drive a live cell into thermal runaway, overcharge or over-discharge to prove a limit. Limits are proven with simulated signals, the simulator, a heated dummy, and the supervised 47 Ω resistor of Gate 11.
  • Never solder to a cell terminal. Reject any cell that is swollen, dented, rusty, leaking, warm or smelling.
  • Work on a non-conductive bench with eye protection, rings and watches off, every exposed terminal covered except the one being worked on, and a lithium-battery fire plan and sand bucket at hand.
  • The hardware BMS stays connected as the final backstop; nothing in the firmware overrides it.
  • The enclosure is vented and is not a fire enclosure: never seal it.
